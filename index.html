About Me & News

Hi everyone, I'm Lorenzo Lausi.

I am a BSc Graduate in Mathematical Sciences for Artificial Intelligence (SMIA) at Sapienza University of Rome (110/110 cum laude).

Academically, I am deeply fascinated by Learning Theory. What fascinates me most about Deep Learning is its generative capability: the possibility of creating entirely new data from the knowledge extracted during learning. For me, this makes Learning Theory essential to understanding and improving generative models. I had the opportunity to thoroughly investigate this theme during my thesis, aiming to understand in which specific regimes an effective learning process occurs within modern neural networks. By defining the parameters that characterize this concept, I observed how the quality of generation strictly depends on the underlying learning process, investigating all these concepts through tools derived from statistical mechanics and complex systems theory.

Research Interests & Academic Projects

Research Interests & Technical Focus

During my studies, I have become increasingly interested in understanding how learning emerges in complex artificial systems, particularly in Deep Generative Models. My recent work has focused on Diffusion Models, studying their learning dynamics through the combined perspectives of Statistical Learning Theory, Optimization, and Statistical Mechanics.

My current research investigates how learning depends on different aspects of model and training dynamics, such as architecture, parameter count, learning rate, and training duration, and how these factors shape the resulting generalization and generative capabilities.

Core Areas

  • Statistical Learning Theory & Optimization: Understanding generalization, learning dynamics, and how optimization shapes the behavior of complex models.
  • Statistical Mechanics of Learning: Studying learning as an emergent phenomenon through the mathematical framework of statistical physics and complex systems.
  • Information Theory & Learning: Investigating how information is extracted and represented, and how relevant structure can be distinguished from irrelevant variability.
  • Deep Generative Models: Understanding how learned representations of complex data distributions enable models to generalize and generate new configurations.
  • Mathematical Modeling & Dynamical Systems: Using mathematical models, stochastic processes, and differential equations to describe learning and complex systems.
  • Signal Processing & Scientific Machine Learning: Exploring machine learning methods for extracting structure from complex and noisy physical signals.

Toward a Theory of Learning

My broader research interest is the development of a general theory of learning: a mathematical framework capable of explaining how artificial and biological systems extract structure from information and develop internal representations of the underlying phenomena.

From this perspective, I see Information Theory as a fundamental language for understanding learning. A learning system receives information about a complex underlying structure, and the central question is how this information is transformed into an internal representation that preserves relevant structure while discarding irrelevant variability.

This perspective naturally connects information, representation, generalization, and generation. Statistical Learning Theory provides a framework for understanding how the structure captured by a learned representation allows a model to generalize beyond the specific observations from which it learned. In generative models, this ability to generalize is ultimately reflected in the capacity to produce new configurations consistent with the learned structure.

I am particularly interested in studying these connections through the combination of Statistical Learning Theory, Information Theory, Statistical Mechanics, and Deep Generative Models, with the broader goal of understanding learning as a dynamical and emergent phenomenon.
