About Me

Hi everyone, I'm Lorenzo Lausi.

I am a BSc Graduate in Mathematical Sciences for Artificial Intelligence (SMIA) at Sapienza University of Rome (110/110 cum laude).

Academically, I am deeply fascinated by Learning Theory. What fascinates me most about Deep Learning is its generative capability: the possibility of creating entirely new data from the knowledge extracted during learning. For me, this makes Learning Theory essential to understanding and improving generative models. I had the opportunity to thoroughly investigate this theme during my thesis, aiming to understand in which specific regimes an effective learning process occurs within modern neural networks. By defining the parameters that characterize this concept, I observed how the quality of generation strictly depends on the underlying learning process, investigating all these concepts through tools derived from statistical mechanics and complex systems theory.

Research Interests & Technical Focus

During my studies, I have become increasingly fascinated by Learning Theory and the generative capabilities of Deep Learning—the power to create something new from acquired knowledge.

Specifically, my current research focuses on finding ways to explain how optimizers learn through the lens of Statistical Learning Theory. I am investigating how the Test Loss varies as a function of different hyperparameters—such as architecture choice, parameter count, learning rate, and training duration—in complex architectures like Diffusion Models.

My core areas of investigation include:

  • Statistical Learning Theory & Optimization: Analyzing how optimizers learn and how Test Loss scales with hyperparameters in Generative AI.
  • Statistical Mechanics & Deep Learning: Analytical explanation of neural networks and understanding their learning processes through statistical physics.
  • Mathematical Modeling & PDEs: Study and application of Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) within AI models.
  • Signal Processing & Denoising: Exploration of SOTA generative approaches operating in the time-frequency domain, with a specific focus on denoising techniques.
